05 — A FAMILY JOURNEY

Across borders.
Still together.

Family testimony says that around 1890 Joseph Obadiah Bibi, his wife Farha, and his brother Salim (Solomon) escaped Baghdad and settled in Damascus. Joseph and his son Reuben remained there until about 1910; Farha, the other children, and Salim with his children stayed until about 1920.[9]

In the family account, Farha and the children arrived in 1920 on the same ship as Hacham Murad and Sarah Maslaton and their children. Salim and his descendants are included here in the story, but not in the direct-ancestor tree. A later family-business history says Joseph and Reuben came from France to California for the World’s Fair during the 1910s, then settled in the United States and moved to New York.[9] [12]

A Sephardic Heritage Museum post excerpt also recalls Reuben Bibi studying in Beirut in the early 1900s. The public preview is truncated, so the archive records only that brief recollection without inferring further details.[13]

From Monastir
to Jerusalem.

A Monastir community history says David Pardo arrived in Jerusalem in 1814, aged about 20, and worked as a miller. It attributes the account to the 1839 Montefiore census. His son Haim Pinhas, born in Jerusalem in 1822, became a rabbi and preacher. The post says Haim married Yael, daughter of Rabbi Shemuel Meyuhas, and that they had three children: Malka, David, and Shemuel Eliezer. The supplied tree independently records Haim as Shemuel Eliezer’s father.[14] [10]

The post remembers Shemuel Eliezer as a leading preacher and educator who taught in the Sephardic Talmud Torah for about 30 years. It also credits him and his brother David with helping found Mishkenot Yisrael in 1875, and says a floor of his home became the Ohel Shemuel synagogue. These community details are presented as the post’s account.[14]
